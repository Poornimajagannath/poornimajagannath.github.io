---
title: Buying back time with AI agents
description: What a small Slack fleet of AI agents is teaching me about reclaiming time, staying present, and shipping imperfect systems.
---

It started with a simple question. Where am I losing time, and what would I do with that time if I got it back?

I spend a lot of time thinking about how AI is changing developer workflows — how to make complex APIs easier to use, how to reduce friction, and how to help someone get from zero to a working integration faster. That curiosity leaked into my personal life. I started looking at the places where I was spending time on planning, sorting, remembering, and deciding. The answers were easy. Dinner walks with my family. Stories at bedtime. Making up songs and singing them badly. Tinkering with new ideas. Being a parent, a partner, and a person who is still figuring things out.

So I started building with the tools in front of me.

I run a fleet of agents on a local GPU. Each one has a clear job. Each one is sandboxed. Each one also has a personality, because that makes it easier for me to know when to use it and what kind of help to expect. There is a family assistant. There is a curriculum assistant that helps me think about what to teach my daughter. There is a health assistant that helps me look at my health metrics in the morning. There is a stock market analyzer. My favorite is Sherlock.

Sherlock is my deep research assistant. The workflow is simple. I send Sherlock screenshots of things I see and want to understand. Sherlock researches them, breaks them down, and builds a report. I read those reports at bedtime. It has become one of the better habits I have built in a while.

All of them live in Slack. They run around the clock. Some of them can talk to each other when a task needs more than one agent. I interact with them the way I would interact with a small team, except the team is there to help me think, plan, and follow through.

Getting here was not clean. I failed a bunch of times before anything actually ran. There were architecture problems I could not solve alone, so I used stronger models to help me think through them. Things broke in ways I did not expect. Agents could not find each other. Nothing coordinated. I stopped trying to make the system perfect before using it. I shipped something rough. I fixed it. I shipped again.

Now I am using the same system to plan birthday parties, which is probably the least glamorous use of agent infrastructure. But we have a lot of them coming up. There are gifts to find, parties to coordinate, and not much margin for error in a summer that is already moving fast.

The Pacific Northwest summers are short. I want to be present for them. That is why I built this. I wanted to buy back a few hours a week and spend them on the things I care about.

So far, it is working.
