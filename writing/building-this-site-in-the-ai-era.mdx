I have always wanted to build a personal website.

Not a resume dump. Not a perfect brand system. Just a place that felt like me. Somewhere I could publish when my mind was clear, not only when I happened to be at a desk.

The AI era made that feel possible in a way it did not before.

I am a mom. I like being outdoors. A lot of my best thinking happens while I am walking, or after bedtime, or in the small gaps where my hands are busy and my head is free. So I started working with voice notes. I talk. Agents listen. We turn the mess into something I can shape later.

That became the workflow.

## Dogfooding my own learning

One way I have been dogfooding what I am learning is simple. I make a list of projects I want to tackle. Then I actually try to do them.

I try to think like an architect. Sometimes I work backwards from the outcome. I ask what the value is. I think about the path from idea to use, just enough to know why it should exist. Then I exercise the muscle that needs a little love. The engineering part.

I use skills to grill me. I identify the architecture. I build prototypes. I evaluate. I get different models to cross-check each other. None of that is glamorous. It is just how I keep learning without waiting for a perfect stretch of uninterrupted time.

## From notes to a site

All of that led me here.

I started by trying to design the site with agents. I experimented with Design.md style prompts and a few structured design passes. That helped me think, but it did not give me the feeling I wanted. So I went back to the pen and paper way, in quotes. I looked at colors, made variations, and kept asking what felt like me.

That is how the calm theme came together. Soft color, enough movement to feel alive, and no giant resume energy. I also wanted a dark mode because I am a developer experience person. Yes, I know developers love dark mode.

For the build, [Astro](https://astro.build/) made sense because it is built for content-driven sites and keeps the page fast. [Blume](https://useblume.dev/) made sense because it let me keep writing in Markdown while still getting a production-grade site with navigation, theme, search, Open Graph images, and AI-readable output. That mattered to me. I wanted the writing to be easy for people to read and easy for AI tools to read.

Then [Codex](https://openai.com/codex/) helped me turn all of that into something real. I could describe the copy, the layout, the tone, and the changes I wanted. Then I could review, reject, rewrite, and keep going.

This site came out of voice notes, Codex, Markdown, Astro, Blume, and a lot of editing.
